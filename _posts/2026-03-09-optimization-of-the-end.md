A night when sleep came early. I fell asleep at 10.

Around 2:30 in the morning, my eyes opened, and a panorama unfolded in my mind.  
How much pride do I truly take in the decisions and choices I have made throughout my life? And where does that pride come from?

**Job 8:7**  
> “Though your beginning was small, your latter days will be very great.”

Optimizing the beginning is efficient. A good start certainly matters. Many beginnings come immediately after the end of another journey, and before we realize it, we find ourselves settling into a stable rhythm.

Yet this seemingly obvious logic of the world does not hold in the kingdom of heaven. A beginning may be great while the end becomes small, and a beginning may be small while the end becomes great.

I want to challenge myself to live by this principle of the kingdom of heaven. As the choices I have made in the past passed through my mind, it felt like watching the landscape through the window of a fast-moving train. The scenes blur together as they pass, yet among them there is always one moment that stands out with striking clarity.

When my attitude toward those choices begins to change, and as those moments gradually fade into the distance, I make a declaration in prayer: that the ends of those choices would be great. And that the great endings of those choices would gather together, so that the final chapter of my life may be holy before God.

**Matthew 20:16**  
> “So the last will be first, and the first will be last.”
